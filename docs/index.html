Pay people,
not just numbers.

Zenbit is a UPI payments concept that shows who is getting your money and how much, keeps your balance private, and keeps ads out of the payment.

See the screens View the code on GitHub

Concept app. People, balances, payments and offers are sample data. No bank is connected, no money moves and no user data is collected.

Why it works

Designed around the moment of paying.

Designed and developed over two weeks, up to 26 November 2026. Written in SwiftUI.

“Am I paying the right person?”

Face, name and UPI ID before paying. Face and amount again while sending.

A face is quicker to check than a string of characters.

“Did it go through?”

A large Dynamic Island card goes from a spinner to a green tick, followed by a sound and a receipt.

One place to look, told through words, colour and sound.

“Don’t show my balance to others.”

No balance before paying. “Not enough balance” appears only after the PIN.

The screen stays safe in a shop queue.

“Stop me before a mistake.”

Warnings for repeated payments and suspicious requests.

The check happens before money leaves.

“Make the usual thing quick.”

Suggested amount, Face ID, favourites and widgets.

Paying someone again takes 3 taps from Home.

“Keep ads out of my way.”

Ads are labelled and never appear in the pay flow or receipt.

The business earns without getting between a person and a payment.

UI decisions I made

Recognisable, and quick to open.

Removed the extra “+₹50” chip.

Less competes with the main actions.

A small see-through Scan button

Narrow floating pill with a green icon.

In reach without hiding content.

No Google sign-in, a labelled step bar and clear OTP and PIN screens.

A real iOS-style number pad

Round keys, letters and even spacing.

Real logos, compact “Pay from”

Bank and merchant logos with less text.

Privacy for low balance

The message moved to after the PIN.

How the business works

Free payments build the habit. Services pay for it.

The model assumes person-to-person UPI payments earn nothing. Revenue would come from useful services around them.

Free UPI paymentsThe reason to come back
TrustClear, private, predictable
ServicesAds, vouchers, bills, credit, gold and insurance
Limited rewardsCashback within what the business can afford
Planned streamWho paysWhy they would pay
Sponsored adsBrandsTo reach people who are spending
Brand-funded vouchersBrandsTo win a new customer
Bills and rechargesBillers and aggregatorsA commission per bill, where offered

No partner is signed. Each stream would need commercial agreements and regulatory checks.

Data

What is measured, and what isn’t yet.

The app has event hooks that write only to the device’s system log. There is no analytics service or server, so there is no user data to report.

Not measured yet: taps per task, time per step, drop-off rates and ad impressions. A real launch would add these with consent and an off switch, and never record PINs, balances, amounts, UPI IDs or names.

Revenue model

An illustration, not results.

Cashback decides whether it works.

The app shows 0.5% back on every payment. On ₹8,000 of eligible spend that costs ₹40 a user, against ₹16.90 earned. Under these assumptions, a launch should cap cashback, let brands fund bigger rewards, or stay below about 0.21%.
